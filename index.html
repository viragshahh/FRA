METHODOLOGY RATIONALE

Why this universe and these dates?

Why 45 stocks?

The 45 company universe is a practical screening set across multiple industries. It is large enough to compare different risk profiles while remaining manageable for consistent price cleaning, return calculation and covariance analysis. It is a purposive research universe, not a random sample of every Indian listed company, so conclusions apply to this selected set.

Why price history from 2018?

Starting in 2018 gives a common historical base before the 2020 shock and allows the same companies to be compared across the selected regimes. A common date range reduces differences caused simply by using different histories for different stocks.

Why adjusted closing prices?

Adjusted prices account for corporate actions such as splits and dividends more consistently than unadjusted closes, making them better suited to estimating investor returns. Missing or zero prices must be treated as missing data rather than real prices.

Why use the Nifty 50?

The Nifty 50 provides a broad market reference for interpreting beta and market conditions. It is a benchmark, not a claim that the selected 45 stocks exactly represent the index.

WHY THESE METRICS AND WEIGHTS?

Three different views of risk

Beta · 30%

Beta measures how sensitively a stock tends to move with the Nifty 50. A 30% weight gives market related risk meaningful influence without letting it dominate the score, because beta does not capture all company specific volatility or the full severity of losses.

Annualized volatility · 40%

Volatility measures the overall dispersion of returns and is the most direct broad measure of total return variability among our three inputs. We give it the largest weight because the study compares overall portfolio risk across different market conditions, not only market sensitivity or the single worst decline.

Maximum drawdown · 30%

Maximum drawdown measures the largest peak to trough decline in the observed price path. A 30% weight adds a clear downside experience measure that volatility and beta cannot show on their own, while keeping it balanced with the other two risk dimensions.

04 • COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

Portfolio and market regimes

We compare the same portfolios across a stress period, a calmer period and a rolling window. These historical outcomes describe the sample; they do not define permanent portfolio characteristics.

WHY THESE MARKET REGIMES?

Stress, calm and recent conditions

2020 · Crisis

We use 2020 because the COVID 19 shock produced the most severe observed risk conditions in our selected comparison, including the highest volatility and other downside risk indicators in the study. This makes it a useful stress test for how the two portfolios behaved during a sharp market disruption.

2023 · Calm comparison

We use 2023 because the selected portfolios show their lowest risk readings in this comparison, including lower volatility and downside risk measures than the crisis window. “Calm” is therefore a relative label based on our sample metrics, not a claim that every day in 2023 was quiet.

Latest 18 months · Recent window

We use 1 April 2025 to 1 October 2026 rather than only the latest 12 months because the 12 month window was not considered a useful benchmark for this analysis: it showed negative returns and gave a narrower view of recent conditions. The additional six months provide more observations and a broader recent sample. This is a design choice, and negative returns alone do not make a period statistically invalid; the reason for preferring 18 months is to avoid basing the recent comparison on that short, unusually weak window alone.

05 • MODEL CHECK

VaR backtesting

Backtesting compares predicted loss thresholds with realized returns. An exception occurs when the actual loss exceeds the forecast threshold.

We assess model calibration by comparing realized exceptions with the rate implied by each confidence level. A rejected Kupiec test indicates that observed exception frequency differs significantly from the model-implied rate at the 5% significance level; a non-rejection is not proof that the model is correct.

WHY THIS BACKTEST?

Observed exceptions are a direct model check

The Kupiec proportion of failures test compares the observed share of VaR breaches with the rate implied by the confidence level. It is used because VaR makes a testable frequency claim: about 5 percent of observations may breach a 95 percent threshold and about 1 percent may breach a 99 percent threshold when the model is correctly calibrated. The test evaluates unconditional exception frequency only. It does not test whether breaches cluster in time, and the 99 percent test can have limited power when the sample is short.

06 • MEAN VARIANCE FRAMEWORK

Markowitz optimization

The constrained optimizer minimizes annualized portfolio variance while respecting a minimum holding weight and an expected return floor.

WHY THIS OPTIMIZATION SETUP?

Make the objective and constraints interpretable

Why minimize variance?

The Markowitz framework links portfolio expected return to the weighted asset means and portfolio variance to the covariance matrix. Minimizing variance subject to a return floor asks for the least volatile allocation that meets a stated return requirement.

Why use the covariance matrix?

Individual stock volatility alone misses how stocks move together. Covariance captures those joint movements and is central to the diversification benefit in mean variance optimization.

Why keep a 1 percent minimum weight?

The floor prevents the optimizer from silently dropping a selected holding entirely while still allowing allocations to differ meaningfully from the equal weight baseline. It is a design constraint for this exercise, not a universal investment rule.

Why use equal weight as the baseline?

Ten percent per stock is simple, transparent and easy to reproduce. It gives a clear reference for judging whether optimization changes risk and allocation rather than confusing the result with an arbitrary starting portfolio.

Why use the TTM return as the target?

The TTM equal weight expected return provides a common reference target. Applying that target across regimes makes the comparison more consistent because each optimizer faces a comparable return requirement. Historical feasibility still depends on each regime's means and covariance, so the constraint must be checked for every run.

What is the main limitation?

Mean variance optimization is sensitive to estimated means and covariances. Small input changes can produce large weight changes, and the model does not directly account for trading costs, taxes or changing future relationships.

WHY THIS COMPARISON?

Same holdings, different information sets

Holding the portfolio definition constant isolates how estimated risk and return change across observation windows. This is a retrospective comparison, not a live strategy simulation. Because the selected stocks and composite scores use a broad historical dataset, a strict historical performance claim would require point in time stock selection and parameter estimation using only information available before each test period.

INTERPRETATION

What changes from one window to another?

Because the portfolio selection uses a broad historical dataset, this comparison can contain lookahead bias. A true historical strategy test would need to select stocks and estimate parameters using only information available before each test period.
