METHODOLOGY RATIONALE

Why this universe and these dates?

Why 45 stocks from the NIFTY 100?

The 45 stocks were selected to capture diverse sectoral and risk profiles while ensuring sufficient historical data for consistent comparison across market regimes. This provides a manageable yet diversified universe for risk analysis, covariance estimation, and Markowitz portfolio optimization.

Why price history from 2018?

It provides a common history before the 2020 crisis and supports comparison across market periods.

Why adjusted closing prices?

They account for dividends and stock splits, making return calculations more consistent.

Why use the Nifty 50 benchmark?

It provides a market reference for beta and market movements; the 45 stocks are not meant to replicate the index.

03 • PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION

Risk scoring and stock selection

The study compares two ten stock portfolios formed from a composite score that combines market sensitivity, volatility and drawdown.

METHODOLOGY

How we build the composite risk score

We combine three complementary measures to rank the 45 selected stocks by relative risk.

Beta (30%)

Market sensitivity: how strongly a stock tends to move with the Nifty 50.

Annualized volatility (40%)

Overall return variability. It receives the highest weight because it captures broad fluctuations across market conditions.

Maximum drawdown (30%)

The largest peak-to-trough fall, adding a direct measure of downside experience.

Correlation check and ES exclusion

We compared the candidate metrics across all 45 stocks before finalizing the score. ES 99% correlated very strongly with annualized volatility (r = 0.9557). Including both could double-count similar risk information, so ES is excluded from this composite score—but remains useful for tail-loss analysis elsewhere in the project.

How the three metrics work together

Each metric is converted to a relative score from 0–100, then combined using the weights above:

Composite score = 0.30 × Beta + 0.40 × Volatility + 0.30 × Maximum Drawdown

Beta captures market-linked risk, volatility captures general return variability, and drawdown captures the worst peak-to-trough loss. Together they balance three different risk dimensions. The weights are transparent, judgment-based choices—not statistically optimized parameters. The result is a relative screening tool, not a prediction of future losses.

05 • MODEL CHECK

VaR backtesting

Compare the High Risk and Low Risk portfolios using the Kupiec p-value only.

METHODOLOGY

What is being tested?

The Kupiec test checks whether the observed exception frequency is consistent with the VaR confidence level. At the 5% significance level, p < 0.05 means reject the null hypothesis; p ≥ 0.05 means do not reject it. No independence or conditional-coverage test is used for the interpretation.
